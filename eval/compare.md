## q01: What does the evidence say about SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42730804, 42518892, 42644019*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 53.2s)

SGLT2 inhibitors have been shown to provide clinically meaningful benefits in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). Specifically, the DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced trials demonstrated significant reductions in worsening heart failure and cardiovascular death, regardless of diabetes status [PMID:42730804]. Additionally, subgroup analyses from a systematic review and meta-analysis indicated a greater benefit in HFrEF compared to heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) [PMID:42518892]. These findings support the use of SGLT2 inhibitors in HFrEF patients, as they reduce hospitalizations for heart failure, cardiovascular death, and the composite endpoint of heart failure and death [PMID:42518892]. ## q03: What adverse events, including euglycemic ketoacidosis, are associated with SGLT2 inhibitors?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42729904, 42694226, 42760177, 42587086, 42759692*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 97.0s)

SGLT2 inhibitors are associated with various adverse events, including hypoglycemia, euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis, volume depletion, acute kidney injury, genital and urinary tract infections, bone fractures, and lower limb amputations [PMID:42587086]. In heart failure, the reporting of unexpected serious events such as ketoacidosis, and a higher proportion of fatal outcomes, has been noted [PMID:42760177]. Additionally, genitourinary side effects such as genital mycotic infections, urinary tract infections, and, rarely, serious complications including urosepsis, Fournier's gangrene, and euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis have been observed [PMID:42631022]. A case of euglycemic ketoacidosis in a nondiabetic patient receiving dapagliflozin for heart failure was reported, highlighting the under-recognized nature of this adverse effect [PMID:42729904]. ## q05: What is known about SGLT2 inhibitor use in elderly or frail patients with heart failure?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42694338, 42613674, 42524338, 42521604*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 132.6s)

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have emerged as an important therapy in heart failure (HF), but their safety and efficacy in elderly patients remain underexplored, as this group is often underrepresented in clinical trials [PMID:42694338].

A multicentre, single-arm, retrospective cohort study was conducted from January to December 2023 at two Malaysian hospitals, evaluating the safety outcomes and changes in clinical parameters following the initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors in elderly Malaysian patients with heart failure [PMID:42694338].

In this study, among 126 patients (median age 70 years), acute kidney injury (AKI) was the most frequent adverse event (12.1%), followed by orthostatic hypotension (3.0%) and urinary tract infection (1.5%) [PMID:42694338]. More than half (51.4%) showed left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) improvement, while 38.2% of diabetic patients achieved better glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) control. Renal function improved or was preserved in 40.7%, and 21.4% demonstrated New York Heart Association (NYHA) class improvement [PMID:42694338].

However, given the single-arm retrospective design and the absence of a comparator group, these observed changes cannot be definitively attributed to SGLT2 inhibitor therapy alone and should therefore be interpreted as associations rather than evidence of a causal treatment effect. Careful clinical monitoring remains essential, particularly in patients with underlying renal impairment [PMID:42694338].

## q06: What mechanisms explain the cardioprotective effects of SGLT2 inhibitors, such as cardiac remodeling?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42792704, 42730804, 42645205*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 77.5s)

SGLT2 inhibitors are associated with cardioprotective effects through mechanisms including:

- Cardiac remodeling, such as reverse remodeling of left ventricular volumes and improvements in myocardial function in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) [PMID:42792704].
- Modulation of inflammation, reduction of oxidative stress, and attenuation of maladaptive tissue remodelling [PMID:42814168].
- Improved ionic homeostasis and mitochondrial quality control [PMID:42814168].
- Potential connections to microvascular dysfunction and fibrosis via endothelial injury and endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) [PMID:42814168].

## q07: What does the evidence say about SGLT2 inhibitors combined with GLP-1 receptor agonists for cardiovascular or renal outcomes?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42616235, 42802824, 42802242*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 71.3s)

The evidence suggests that the combined use of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists may provide more favorable cardiorenal outcomes compared to either agent alone. Specifically, the combined use was associated with lower risks of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and hospitalization for heart failure (HHF) [PMID:42616235]. Additionally, it showed a better total estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) slope compared to GLP-1RA monotherapy [PMID:42616235].

However, the findings are based on non-randomized within-trial subgroups and are described as hypothesis-generating, requiring confirmation in dedicated head-to-head randomized trials [PMID:42616235].

## q08: How do SGLT2 inhibitors perform in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)?

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 54.7s)

In a nationwide cohort study of 2177 patients with type 2 diabetes and HFpEF, starting SGLT2 inhibitors was associated with a significantly lower risk of hospital admission for heart failure and all cause mortality compared with starting treatment with DPP4 inhibitors or sulfonylureas [PMID:42644019]. The available evidence supports SGLT2 inhibitors as the pharmacological foundation for managing obesity-driven HFpEF because they provide the most mature outcome data across the preserved ejection fraction spectrum [PMID:42586458]. ## q09: What perioperative or periprocedural considerations apply to SGLT2 inhibitors around surgery?

*Expert-expected PMIDs (auto-suggested, needs review): 42759692, 42739811*

### ollama (qwen2.5:7b, status=answered, 128.6s)

Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are established therapies for type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease, creating a perioperative need to balance prevention of euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) against cardiorenal protection. Fasting, surgical stress, infection, reduced carbohydrate intake, insulin dose reduction, and hypovolaemia can amplify glycosuria and ketogenesis and precipitate euDKA. Most guidelines recommend withholding SGLT2 inhibitors for 3-4 days before elective surgery, although recommendations vary by agent, procedure, fasting duration, diabetes status, and monitoring capacity. Emerging evidence indicates competing perioperative risks and benefits. Inadequate withholding and emergency surgery are associated with high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis and ketoacidosis, whereas observational studies associate preoperative use with lower risks of acute kidney injury and short-term mortality. Heart failure withdrawal studies further suggest that cardiovascular and volume-regulatory benefits may diminish rapidly after interruption. These findings challenge uniform withholding but do not support routine perioperative continuation. Management should be stratified by treatment indication, glycated haemoglobin, insulin dependence, hepatic and kidney function, infection and volume status, surgical urgency and invasiveness, expected fasting duration, and access to ketone and acid-base monitoring. Patients at high metabolic risk generally require an adequate preoperative withholding interval and intensified monitoring, whereas low-risk patients treated primarily for heart failure or chronic kidney disease require individualised multidisciplinary assessment. [PMID:42759692]
